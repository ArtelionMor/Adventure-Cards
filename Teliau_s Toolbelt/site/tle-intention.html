TLE Intention v3.2 SA

What a Time-Limited Event is, how it is configured, and how the sheet drives the live event.

Read in ~10 min

What is a Time-Limited Event?

A Time-Limited Event (TLE) is a temporary, self-contained world the player can visit on top of the main game. It runs on a schedule, has its own difficulty, its own reward gauge, and (sometimes) a leaderboard. When the timer ends, the world disappears — along with any progress not collected.

Feature scorecard (vs other event slots)

From the design Event list table, each event slot is rated on five intention axes (1 = low, 5 = high). The TLE slot scores:

AxisTLEBoss ChallengeArenaWhat it means
Reward targetting531Players can aim at a specific reward they need (biome egg, pet shard, candies).
Engagement depth422Multi-day, multi-world progression — strong reason to come back daily.
Power challenge545Worlds are tuned just above the player's current team power.
New gameplay243Reuses main-world mechanics with modifiers — not a new mode.
Accessible challenge253Deliberately steep — Odysseys are not for Light personas.
Positioning: TLE is the "reward-targetted, engagement-heavy, power-challenging" slot. It is not meant to introduce new mechanics or be accessible to all players — those are roles the Boss Challenge and Arena occupy.

Why we ship TLEs

As a player

I want to engage more to earn more rewards, and I want to target specific rewards I need (a missing biome egg, a specific pet shard, candies).

As a game maker

We want to challenge players on their collection variety (biome restrictions, specific pet boosts) and on their team power (dynamic-power worlds tuned just above the player baseline).

KPI targets

Primary: D14 / D7 retention. Secondary: D7 / D1, redeposit, LTV D7. The event is a recurring reason to come back during the week.

Mental model: a TLE is a scheduled mini-world with its own difficulty knob (Power), its own pacing (α curve), its own collection challenge (modifier), and its own payoff (gauge rewards + optional leaderboard). The sheet is the generator that produces all of these from a single row.

Deconstruction

From the [3.2] Time-limited worlds Balancing Intentions doc. Two deconstructions feed the design: (1) what other games taught us about TLE-style events, and (2) how each main-world reward element converts into event_xp.

1. Reference deconstruction (competitor games)

Each row is a learning lifted from a specific game, with the takeaway we ported into the TLE design.

GameMode / eventWhat they doWhat we take from it
Pocket Champs Events (off-the-Trophy-Road races) Events are solutions to problems — when you're stuck on the Trophy Road, you run an event to unblock yourself. First 3 races are easy to clear; after that it ramps. Restrictions force a different strategy ("can't use the overboard → try the jetpack"). Modifiers offer strategic decisions on which character / gear to bring. "We challenge players on similar difficulties, but we only reward them when they succeed." Side activities are part of the core loop, not a parallel track. Rewards are better than the Trophy Road on victory — but not insanely better, because the goal is a strategic split, not pure addition.
Golds & Gobelins Second-world event The "second world" duplicates everything (potions, coins, cards, upgrades) and the player restarts from zero inside the event. This works because the core loop is idle — relaunching is fine, you fast-forward through the adventure and get blocked faster. The event has its own store with different chests. Restarting from zero in a TLE is acceptable when the core loop is fun. It lets the player try different strategies / upgrade paths. The event store as a separate cosmetic surface is a strong idea.
AFK Journey — overall caveat on the game — Small-scale game. Team-building leans aesthetic rather than strategic; power-driven players flee when the game becomes grindy. Used as a caution, not a pattern: nothing in AFK Journey at the macro level is something we are copying. The two sub-modes below are what we actually borrow from.
Raid Treasure Combat mode restricted to characters of a given role. Rewards craft gear usable only by that role. Daily rotation (Mon: tank, Tue: wizard, …) shifted by 1 day each week. "We challenge on A and reward on A." The daily rotation pattern is exactly what the Island Trial Mon / Wed cadence borrows.
Season Legend Trial Combat mode restricted to characters of a given race. Rewards are exclusive resources used to max-level your characters. Daily rotation (Mon: blues, Tue: reds, …) with combo days at the end of the week. "We challenge on Collection, we reward on Power." Ported to Scroll: challenge the player on biomes (Island Odyssey biome restriction) and reward them with specific rewards they want (biome eggs, candies, new pets).
Net synthesis ported into Scroll TLEs:
  • Island Trial = the Raid Treasure pattern → challenge on Power, reward on Power-relevant items, daily rotation.
  • Island Odyssey = the Season Legend Trial pattern → challenge on Collection (biome restriction), reward on biome-targeted items + leaderboard for Elite.
  • Reward ceiling follows the Pocket-Champs rule: better than the main world, but not so much better that the main world becomes pointless.

2. Element deconstruction (what becomes event_xp?)

Every reward source in the main world is converted into event_xp inside a TLE. The conversion uses the same HC value as the main world, multiplied by the world's XP multiplier.

Conversion rate (working assumption): 1 point of event_xp ≈ value of main_world_xp × 0.1. To be iterated as we observe real player behaviour.

"Multiplier" here is the per-world XP multiplier (W1 = ×1, W2 = ×2, W3 = ×4, W4 = ×8, W5 = ×16 in the canonical Odyssey ramp). Quests and resources are the backbone of the TLE economy; the rest are smaller opportunistic boosts.

3. Reward calibration (62 HC / minute)

From the simulator, the average rentability of the game converges to ≈ 62 HC per minute of play. The TLE rewards are calibrated against this baseline: a Light persona who plays 15 min/day gets the equivalent of 62 × 15 × event_days HC of value out of the event, plus a small leaderboard bonus.

5. Open questions from the doc

  • Should ~20% of main-world rewards be redistributed toward events? Strategic discussion still open — it would change the overall economy balance.
  • First reward must be cheap — clearing the first quest should deliver the first gauge reward, so the rewards progression cannot be linear.
  • Conversion rate of 0.1 between main_world_xp HC value and event_xp is a starting assumption; it will be iterated.
  • Bots on the leaderboard — TLE leaderboards are populated by bots calibrated so a player who logs in 12 times during the event takes 1st place by the deadline.

Behaviour inside the event

The Balancing doc describes how each persona is expected to play the event — useful for sanity-checking that the rebloom cost and the gauge shape are tuned right.

Light

Clears half the map and doesn't come back. Picks up the first gauge reward and moves on. Treats the event as a treasure opportunity, not a goal.

Regular

Clears the map, occasionally returns to re-clear it waiting for the auto-rebloom. Won't pay for a sold rebloom — opportunistic gameplay. Mid-gauge target on a Trial, a couple of worlds on an Odyssey.

Invested

Clears the map and is serious about refreshes ("I know I need to come back in a few hours"). Willing to spend a few reblooms to catch up after missing a cycle, because they still want the final gauge reward.

Elite

Clears everything, often. Pays whatever it takes for leaderboard rewards — within the time budget set out in the engagement table.

How the personas and tiers connect: the persona tiers are population buckets (every player belongs to one); the Bronze / Silver / Gold tiers are leaderboard placements within an event. An Invested player can land in Bronze; an Elite player typically lands in Silver or Gold. The reward bonus is cumulative: an Elite who hits Gold gets the Elite bonus pool and the Gold placement bonus.

What is the TLE Intention tab?

The TLE tab is the design sheet that fully describes a Time-Limited Event: its 5 worlds, the XP gauge the player fills, the rewards handed out at each milestone, the optional leaderboard, the difficulty curve, and the generator output the game actually consumes. One row = one event configuration.

The 4 functional blocks

Parameters

Top-left global knobs (target play time, leaderboard alpha, difficulty growth…) shared by all events.

Gauge

Per-world α curves & total HC / XP, plus the XP multiplier and the list of rewards + targets (1st…20th).

Difficulty

Per-world Power offset, modifier config, expected player-behaviour split (Light / Normal / Engaged / Elite + Bronze / Silver / Gold).

Generator

The structured output the game reads: WorldProgressConfig, WorldConfig, WorldEvent, eventContentList.

From sheet to live event

1. ParametersSet the global play-time & difficulty knobs.
2. GaugeDefine XP / HC totals and reward milestones per world.
3. DifficultySet Power offsets and modifier per world.
4. GeneratorSheet writes the WorldProgress + WorldEvent configs.
5. In-gamePlayers fill the gauge, climb the leaderboard, claim rewards.
Mental model: the sheet is a generator, not just a description. The left columns are the design intent; the right columns (GENERATOR section) are the artefact the engine consumes — they should never be hand-edited.

Gauge & reward curves

Every TLE has a gauge the player fills with XP. The shape of the gauge is controlled by an α (alpha) curve, the total XP / HC per world, and an XP multiplier. Rewards are dropped at 4 to 20 milestones along the gauge; if a leaderboard is enabled, additional reward tiers are stacked on top.

Per-world curve parameters

BlockColumnsWhat it controls
α HC curveW1 → W5Front / back tilt knob for how HC is split across the milestones. The cumulative target stays strictly increasing for every α. 0.1–1 = back-loaded (convex: slow then steep; α = 1 → linear 10–20–30–40 over 4 milestones). 1–5 = front-loaded (concave: steep then plateau; larger α → first milestone dominates).
HC amountW1 → W5Total HC distributed by the gauge in that world.
α XP curveW1 → W5Same role as HC α but applied to the XP curve.
XP amountW1 → W5Total XP needed to fully fill the gauge in that world.
XP multiplierW1 → W5Boost applied on top of base XP rewards (1, 2, 4, 8, 16…).

Two separate reward blocks

Since the v(4) sheet, gauge rewards and leaderboard rewards live in two distinct blocks of 20 cells each:

  • Leaderboard rewards (cols 41–60) + Leaderboard targets (cols 62–81). Tier targets are computed from XP Total, alpha xp and alpha reward using the same t1/α curve. Filled only when Leaderboard ? = TRUE.
  • Gauge rewards (cols 83–102) + Gauge targets (cols 104–123). For an Odyssey, the 20 cells are read as 4 milestones × 5 worlds (slots 1–4 belong to W1, 5–8 to W2, … 17–20 to W5) and the cumulative XP/HC target resets at the start of each world.

Two layers of α, with two roles

WhereColumnsWhat it shapes
Per-world gaugeα HC W1…W5, α XP W1…W5How the HC and XP totals of a single world are split across that world's gauge milestones.
Leaderboardalpha reward, alpha xpHow the leaderboard reward VALUE and the cumulative XP TARGETS are distributed across the 20 leaderboard tiers (with XP Total as the upper bound of the curve).

α curve playground

Each bar shows the cumulative XP target at that milestone (Σᵢ = Δ₁ + Δ₂ + … + Δᵢ). The last bar always equals Total XP. The smaller +Δ label inside each bar is what this milestone added on top of the previous one — and you'll see it shrink over time when α > 1 (front-loaded), even though the cumulative bars keep climbing toward T.

The cumulative target is strictly increasing for every α (each milestone delivers strictly positive XP, so the running total never dips). α only changes the shape of the climb — convex vs concave — not its monotonicity.

α ∈ [0.1, 1] → back-loaded (convex cumulative — slow at first, steep at the end):
Δi = T × i1/α / Σk k1/α   anchor: α = 1, N = 4, T = 100 → 10, 20, 30, 40. Smaller α pushes more of T onto the last milestone.

α ∈ (1, 5] → front-loaded (concave cumulative — steep climb up front, plateau near T):
Δi = T × (N+1−i)α / Σk kα (mirror of the back-loaded formula). Larger α concentrates T on the first few milestones.

In both regimes Σ Δi = T by construction. N controls how finely T is sliced — more milestones = thinner bars, same total.

Difficulty & player behaviour

Difficulty is a small block but it drives whether a player can finish the gauge in time. Each world receives a Power offset on top of the player's current power, optionally a modifier (e.g. modifier_meadow_low), and assumed shares of player profiles.

FieldWhat it does

Dynamic Power, the heart of TLE difficulty

Each TLE is balanced relative to the player, not against a fixed power. That is the role of use_dynamic_power in the Generator block.

  • use_dynamic_power = TRUE — the world's effective power is computed from the player at event start: maxPlayerPower = bonus + sum(top pets at max upgrade), then world_recommended_power = bonus + teamPower + (additional_power × 6).
  • use_dynamic_power = FALSE — the world uses the fixed world_power value from the row.
  • additional_power is the per-world offset that shifts the dynamic baseline (matches the Difficulty Power W1…W5 columns).
  • Dynamic power is locked at event start (when the event first becomes playable for the player) — catching up later in the week doesn't make the event easier.
The persona / leaderboard-tier reward lists are design targets, not in-game caps. They feed the spreadsheet's expected-value formulas and let the designer verify, at a glance, that each persona reaches the intended payout.

Generator output

The right side of the sheet is the artefact the game consumes. It's split into 4 sub-blocks. Each row of the sheet emits one entry per block (a milestone for the WorldProgressConfig, one WorldEvent, one entry in the eventContentList).

WorldProgressConfig

The reward gauge: id, final_currency_target, currency_id, rewards, reward_currency_target.

World Config

The world used by the event: tile / spawner / resource / landmark / enviro / drop / biomes / tile_count / modifier configs.

WorldEvent

Glues a world to a progression: dynamic flags, multipliers, durations, power, behaviour and the 3 view ids (card / rules / popup).

eventContentList

Group of WorldEvents played in order: id, content_id, progression_order.

Three pairs of columns govern how the event scales with the player. Each pair is a boolean + a multiplier / offset.

eventContentList — grouping & ordering

An Odyssey is 5 WorldEvents played in order. The eventContentList block declares that grouping: same id for all 5 entries, one content_id per WorldEvent, and a progression_order (1…5). A leaderboard, when present, is added to the same list with progression_order = 0.

Treat the GENERATOR columns as read-only output. If you need to change something, change the design intent on the left — the right side will be regenerated.

Open design questions

Still being debated (Notion comments on the Island Trial doc):
  • Should the challenge type adapt to the player's profile? Teliau's proposal: target what the player lacks (collection-light players get a collection challenge, power-light players get a power challenge). Arthur's counter: this can feel like the game is punishing your progression — challenge type should be fixed per event slot. Current resolution: fixed challenge type per event, but the main challenge does not preclude secondary challenge dimensions.
  • Is difficulty coupled to challenge type? Agreed: no — challenge type (Power vs Collection) and difficulty (Medium / High) are independent axes.

FAQ

Why is the right side of the sheet so much taller than the left?

The left side has one row per event (design intent). The right side has one row per generated artefact (a milestone, a WorldEvent, an eventContentList entry). One event can emit dozens of generator rows.

What does the World Gauge Rewards Repartition string mean?

A single number (e.g. 4) = that many equal gauge milestones, no leaderboard. A dash list (e.g. 4 - 2 - 4 - 3 - 5) = the first number is the count of gauge milestones; the following numbers split the leaderboard tiers across worlds (or sub-events).

What is α (alpha)?

A front / back tilt knob for the shape of the cumulative-target curve. Every Δi stays strictly positive, so the cumulative target is strictly increasing for every α — only its shape changes. Two regimes:

  • α ∈ [0.1, 1] → back-loaded (convex cumulative; slow at first, steep at the end): Δi = T · i1/α / Σk k1/α. Anchor: α = 1, N = 4, T = 100 → 10, 20, 30, 40. Smaller α → last milestone dominates.
  • α ∈ (1, 5] → front-loaded (concave cumulative; steep climb early, plateau near T): Δi = T · (N+1−i)α / Σk kα. Larger α → first milestone dominates.

In both regimes the bars sum to T. See the playground in the Gauge tab.

Why are there two sets of α columns?

The per-world α HC W1…W5 and α XP W1…W5 columns shape the milestone progression inside a single world's gauge. The event-wide alpha reward and alpha xp columns shape the 20 leaderboard tiers (combined with XP Total as the curve's upper bound). One pair tunes the gauge, the other tunes the leaderboard.

Which sign of Power offset means “easier”?

Positive = easier for the player, negative = harder. So in an Odyssey that ramps up across worlds you typically see W1 slightly positive and the later worlds going more and more negative. Spike challenges or boss rushes use strongly negative values.

What's the difference between use_dynamic_* flags?

use_dynamic_world_coins / use_dynamic_world_progress_currency / use_dynamic_power = TRUE means the engine scales these values dynamically with the player; FALSE means the fixed value next to them is used as-is.

How do I add a new TLE?

Duplicate an existing row, change the Event name, set the reward repartition, decide on the leaderboard, fill in the per-world α / XP / Power values. The GENERATOR columns will populate automatically — verify they make sense before shipping.

What is the rebloom mechanic?

Inside a TLE world, resources don't respawn on their own. The player must pay HC to rebloom the world and regenerate them. This is deliberate: we want short repeated sessions rather than one long grind. The cost is defined in WorldRulesConfig (type = rebloom, value = HC cost).

Generation rules (live-ops contract):

  • On first generation, resources are placed at fixed positions with fixed types — the same layout greets the player every time they enter.
  • Resource progress is preserved across re-entry when possible (nice-to-have: a 50%-eaten resource still shows 50% after leaving and coming back).
  • A rebloom is the only way to respawn resources. Each rebloom is a new random generation; existing resources are deleted before new ones spawn.
  • Random spawner cycles (wheel of fortune, gifts, …) reset on rebloom — but previously spawned random items already on the map are kept.
What's the difference between a modifier and a restriction?

A modifier changes the rules in the player's favour (e.g. Pet rarity boost, Specific pet boost). A restriction limits what the player can bring (e.g. Biome restriction = only pets from a given biome). Both are referenced via world_modifier_config_id.

Why are personas in the doc named differently from the sheet?

The product docs sometimes use Light / Regular / Invested / Elite; the sheet uses Light / Normal / Engaged / Elite. Same buckets, different wording. The Player personas tab spells out the mapping.
